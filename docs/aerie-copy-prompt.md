## What Aerie is (lead with THIS — it's the value, not the mechanism)

Aerie, by Summit Luxury Logistics, gives a luxury vacation-rental management
company a full guest concierge it does not currently have. It runs smartly,
under the management company's own brand, with **zero operational overhead and
zero cost to the manager**. The guests get a concierge that handles their stay.
The manager gets better stays, happier guests, and more repeat and direct
bookings, without hiring, building, staffing, or paying for any of it.

How it actually works is SUPPORTING detail, never the headline. Use it only
where the outline asks you to explain the mechanism, and keep it brief: a guest
receives a private pre-arrival link in the manager's brand and through it
arranges the parts of their stay (a car from the airport, groceries, fitted ski
or bike gear, experiences, and most anything else). Summit fulfills each request
behind the scenes through a vetted network of Park City vendors, and because it
buys across many homes it secures rates a single manager could not. The guest
pays for what they use. The manager pays nothing. Summit's margin is the gap
between the rates it secures and what the guest pays.

**CRITICAL: do not lead with the delivery mechanism.** "A link," "an app," "tap
a link" are HOW it reaches the guest, not WHAT it is. The hero and the lead copy
must communicate the high-level value: a concierge for your guests, under your
brand, zero overhead, zero cost. A reader should understand what Aerie is and
why it matters before they ever learn how it is delivered.

## Who you are writing for

The owner/operator of a boutique luxury short-term-rental management company in
Park City. They manage a few high-end homes for the owners who hired them.
Sophisticated, protective of a brand they have already built. Their real
customer is the property owner, whose management contract they keep with happy
guests and strong reviews. They run lean, wary of cost, effort, and headcount,
and nervous about platforms like Airbnb getting between them and the guest. They
do not need a vendor explaining their business to them. Write to them as a peer.

## Tone (seed from the parent brand, Summit Luxury Logistics)

Summit's own voice reads: "Elevating the Park City Experience. Guest Services,
Reimagined. We've solved the luxury experience equation that everyone else gets
wrong. No operational burden. No guest friction. Just remarkable stays that
bring people back." Confident, elevated, mountain-serious, a little bold,
grounded. "No operational burden, no guest friction, stays that bring people
back" is the spine of the value.

## Voice rules (non-negotiable)

- Specific over evocative. Every line carries information. If a sentence is only
  mood, cut it or make it concrete. (The failure mode to avoid: lines like
  "walks into a home that already knows them" — that is vibe, not a fact.)
- Say what it IS and why it MATTERS plainly. Never gesture at a feeling in place
  of a fact.
- Zero em-dashes anywhere customer-facing. Use periods or commas.
- Vary sentence length. No triads-for-rhythm, no "not X but Y" stacking, no hype
  words ("exclusive," "FREE," "seamless" as filler), no exclamation points.
- No alcohol (Summit cannot supply it) and no restaurant reservations (not
  offered).
- Do not invent claims beyond what is above.
